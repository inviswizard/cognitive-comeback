Exercise instructions and examples

A · One-listen comprehension — 15 min

Listen to a new explanation once (5 min). Close it and explain aloud (3 min). Draw a diagram or concept map (3 min). Compare against the original and correct mistakes (4 min). Why: practice focused comprehension and reconstruction. Example: explain why a larger hydraulic piston exerts greater force without creating energy. Score 0–5 important points.

B · Memory encoding and recall — 15 min

Read a short unfamiliar story once (3 min). Hide it and write all remembered details (4 min). Check accuracy (3 min). Recall yesterday's story without rereading (5 min). On Day 1, use the final five minutes to make a checklist. Why: practice attention, encoding, and delayed retrieval. Example: Arun visits a bookstore Tuesday, wants a maths guide, finds an astronomy book, forgets his wallet, and asks the shopkeeper to hold it until Friday. Recall the facts without peeking.

C · First-principles reasoning — 20 min

Problem 1: predict and draw (2 min), reason (5 min), check assumptions (2 min). Problem 2: attempt (7 min). Compare governing principles (4 min). Why: test predictions against physical laws rather than guess. Example: in a tank accelerating right, settled water rises on the left; the surface slope magnitude is a/g.

D · Mental visualization — 10 min

Mentally rotate a colored cube: three single rotations (3 min), two combined rotations (3 min), mentally trace a moving object (2 min), check physically (2 min). Why: practice spatial transformations useful for vectors, 3D geometry, optics, and mechanics. A cube score is not an IQ score.

E · Delayed recall and error correction — 15 min

Reconstruct Exercise A's concept (5 min). Correct yesterday's reasoning errors (5 min). Record scores and schedule next recall (5 min). Example: derive normality = molarity × reaction-specific n-factor; for complete neutralization, 0.5 M H₂SO₄ corresponds to 1 N.

JEE learning cycle — inside your study blocks

Preview → Learn → Reconstruct → Apply → Diagnose → Retrieve. Example: derive M·aCM = Fexternal, explain why internal forces cancel, solve two-body examples, then transfer to a person walking on a frictionless plank. For mistakes, classify: missing principle, failed recall, wrong visualization, overcomplication, calculation, or time pressure.

Exercise A · One-listen comprehension (15 minutes) — full instructions
Why

Practice building a correct explanation after one exposure, checking comprehension rather than familiarity. This helps you identify missing prerequisite knowledge.

0–5 minutes: Listen

Pick an unfamiliar 5-minute educational explanation (physics, technology, biology). Put your phone face down if audio-only. Do not pause, rewind, or take detailed notes.

5–8 minutes: Reconstruct

Close the source. Explain it aloud as though teaching a younger student. Start with the main claim, then explain each causal step. Say 'I don't know' where needed.

8–11 minutes: Diagram

Draw a concept map with arrows and labels, or sketch the physical mechanism from memory. Do not look at the original.

11–15 minutes: Check

Reopen the source. Compare against five key points and score 0–5. Write one sentence correcting each missed idea.

Example: Hydraulic brakes transmit pressure through an enclosed fluid. Since F = P × A, a larger piston can exert more force, but moves less distance for a fixed volume. Ask why air bubbles interfere and why this does not create energy.

Do not: replay repeatedly before testing. If accuracy is low, use clearer material, not faster audio. JEE transfer: reconstruct a derivation and test it on a new problem.

Exercise B · Story memory (15 minutes) — full instructions
Why

Practice attention, encoding of factual details, and delayed retrieval. It is not a treatment for forgetting autobiographical events.

0–3 minutes: Encode

Generate a new story above. Read it once, or listen to the narrator with the text hidden. Notice the sequence: person → place → intention → unexpected event → outcome.

3–7 minutes: Retrieve

Hide the story. Write every detail you remember in the recall box without prompts.

7–10 minutes: Verify

Show questions, answer mentally, then reveal the key. Count correct factual details. Note whether you forgot names, time, place, sequence, or cause.

10–15 minutes: Delay

Recall yesterday's story without opening it first. On Day 1, review omissions and write a checklist for tomorrow.

Example: Arun visited a bookstore on Tuesday, intended to buy a maths guide, found an astronomy book, forgot his wallet, and the shopkeeper held the book until Friday. Try to retrieve all five facts after a delay.

Do not: confuse recognition after rereading with genuine recall. JEE transfer: recall definitions, formulas and mechanisms from the previous day before checking notes.

Exercise C · First-principles reasoning (20 minutes) — full instructions
Why

Practice accurate prediction, causal reasoning, and applying familiar laws to unfamiliar setups.

0–2 minutes

Read a conceptual problem. Draw your first prediction and label forces, motions or constraints. Commit to an answer before checking.

2–7 minutes

Reason from governing principles. List assumptions (e.g. frictionless, closed system, constant acceleration). Work through equations only after your physical prediction.

7–9 minutes

Check the solution. Record what made your prediction right or wrong.

9–16 minutes

Repeat with a second unfamiliar question.

16–20 minutes

Compare the two questions. State which underlying principles are shared and which differ.

Example: A partially filled tank accelerates right. In the tank's steady accelerating frame, the water surface is higher on the left. The slope magnitude satisfies tan θ = a/g. Predict before calculating.

Do not: equate lucky guesses with understanding. JEE transfer: identify governing laws before searching for memorized solution patterns.

Exercise D · Mental visualization (10 minutes) — full instructions
Why

Practice mentally transforming shapes and simulating physical arrangements; transfer is most plausible when you apply it to actual diagrams.

0–3 minutes

Mark six faces of a paper cube with different colors. Mentally perform three separate 90° rotations and predict the top/front/right faces.

3–6 minutes

Try two sequences of rotations, for example rotate around the vertical axis then the horizontal axis. Keep track of orientation.

6–8 minutes

Imagine a moving object, reflected ray, or vector rotating through a diagram. Sketch the final arrangement.

8–10 minutes

Verify with the real cube or drawing. Record number correct out of five transformations.

Example: Predict the components of a vector after a 90° counterclockwise rotation in the xy-plane; (x,y) becomes (−y,x).

Do not: treat a cube score as an IQ score. JEE transfer: vectors, 3D geometry, optics and rotation.

Exercise E · Delayed retrieval and error correction (15 minutes) — full instructions
Why

Make recall more durable and turn mistakes into a specific correction plan.

0–5 minutes

Without notes, reconstruct the main idea from Exercise A. State its mechanism and one example.

5–10 minutes

Review yesterday's mistakes. Label each as missing principle, failed recall, incorrect mental model, calculation, or time pressure. Correct the underlying reason.

10–15 minutes

Record today's scores. Schedule tomorrow's recall and select one principle to retrieve again in a few days.

Example: Derive N = M × n-factor for a specified reaction. In complete neutralization, H₂SO₄ has n-factor 2, so 0.5 M is 1 N.

Do not: copy notes and count it as retrieval. JEE transfer: spaced recall, formula derivations and error logs.

JEE study · Four 60-minute blocks and a learning cycle

Physics: choose one principle, reconstruct the derivation, solve problems and classify errors. Math: derive a method and apply it to varied problems. Chemistry: understand one concept or reaction framework, then practice. Mixed block: interleave older topics and test retrieval. Within each hour use about 45–50 focused minutes and a short break.

Six-stage method: Preview → Learn → Reconstruct → Apply → Diagnose → Retrieve. Example: derive M aCM = Fexternal, explain cancellation of internal forces, then transfer to a person walking on a frictionless plank.

Measure: accuracy, reasoning, time, and mistake type. Don't chase speed until accuracy is reliable.

Sleep, exercise, meals, and daily rhythm — exact implementation

Sleep: aim for 8–10 hours actual sleep; a suggested 23:00–08:00 sleep opportunity is nine hours in bed. Start wind-down at 22:00. Shift gradually if your schedule differs. Exercise: work toward an average of 60 minutes daily activity across the week, such as two 30-minute walks; increase gradually if needed. Food: regular balanced meals with carbohydrates, protein, fruit/vegetables, and adequate water. Avoid all-nighters, extreme training, fasting, and stimulant-based cramming.

Suggested order: 08:00 wake + daylight → breakfast and movement → 75-minute drills → Physics → Math → lunch → Chemistry → mixed JEE → afternoon movement → dinner → wind-down → 23:00 lights out.

Seven-day goals and scoring — what success means

Day 1: baseline using moderate unfamiliar tasks. Day 2: encoding and next-day story recall. Day 3: first-pass comprehension. Day 4: visual reasoning and predictions. Day 5: transfer principles to differently worded problems. Day 6: timed mixed JEE execution without sacrificing accuracy. Day 7: retest with new matched-difficulty material.

Comprehension: correct key points / 5. Memory: correct details / total asked. Reasoning: correct conceptual questions / attempted. Also record actual sleep and alertness. Compare trends cautiously: one week cannot guarantee recovery or establish a medical diagnosis.
